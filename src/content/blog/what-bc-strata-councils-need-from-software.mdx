If you've served on a strata council in British Columbia, you know the job is never just the meetings. It's the "where are the 2019 minutes?" emails, the same bylaw question for the fifth time this month, and the scramble to assemble an AGM package.

We built [OpenStrata](https://openstrata.ca) to take that load off volunteer councils. Here's what councils actually need, and what to look for in strata management software, whether or not you choose ours.

## 1. One searchable home for strata documents

Bylaws, rules, minutes, insurance summaries, depreciation reports and engineering reports tend to live across a past treasurer's laptop, a shared drive and someone's inbox. The single most valuable thing software can do is put all of it in **one place, searchable, organized by year and topic**.

**Look for:** full-text search, organization by year or topic, and AGM packages that stay together as a set.

## 2. Clear access control between council and residents

Not every document is for everyone. Councils need to share bylaws and approved minutes with all owners and residents while keeping legal correspondence and draft budgets within council.

**Look for:** per-document or per-folder visibility, and the ability to change it without asking a vendor.

## 3. A request system, not an inbox

Resident requests about a leaking pipe, a parking question or a move-in booking usually land in one volunteer's personal email. Things get lost when that volunteer goes on vacation or steps down.

**Look for:** requests that route to the right person automatically, a visible status, and a history that survives council turnover.

## 4. Self-service answers

Most resident questions repeat. When residents can find the answer themselves, such as move-in rules, pet bylaws or the amenity room booking process, council members get their evenings back.

## 5. No app to install

Adoption is everything. If residents need to download an app and create yet another account, many won't. Browser-based software that works on any phone or computer removes the biggest barrier.

## 6. A pilot before the rollout

Councils are rightly cautious about changing how a whole building communicates. The safest path is to start with **council only**, load the documents, try the workflows, and then invite residents with guided onboarding.

## 7. Built for BC

Strata governance in British Columbia has its own vocabulary and rhythms: the Strata Property Act, AGMs and SGMs, depreciation reports, and the relationship between council, owners and property managers. Software designed for BC strata from the start fits better than a generic tool adapted for it.
