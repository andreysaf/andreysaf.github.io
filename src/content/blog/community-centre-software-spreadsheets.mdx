Most community centres, family centres and drop-in programs run on some combination of a paper sign-in sheet, a spreadsheet on the front desk computer, and a staff member who remembers everyone's name. That works, until it doesn't.

I built [OpenCommunity](https://opencommunity.ca) to focus my industry experience on the communities I'm part of, here in British Columbia. It's designed around the places where that setup tends to fall apart, in roughly this order.

## What breaks first: sign-in

A clipboard at reception is fast for visitors and slow for everyone else. Someone has to type the names into a spreadsheet later. Handwriting is unreadable, names are spelled three different ways, and the same family appears as four different people.

**What works instead:** a reception kiosk where returning visitors sign in with a phone number and a short PIN. Attendance is recorded against the right program at the moment it happens, and nobody re-types anything.

## What breaks next: funder reporting

Funders and boards ask reasonable questions:

- How many unique families did you serve this quarter?
- What's the demographic breakdown?
- Which programs grew?

With spreadsheets, answering takes days of deduplicating and cross-referencing, and the numbers are still approximate.

**What works instead:** reporting built on clean, deduplicated records. You get attendance summaries, demographic breakdowns, unique-family counts and per-program statistics in minutes, not days.

## Then: scheduling and rosters

Recurring drop-ins, weekly groups and specialist visits each need a roster. Copying last week's spreadsheet tab is error-prone, and staff can't easily see who is expected.

**What works instead:** recurring sessions that generate themselves, with rosters attached.

## Then: more than one location

Once an organization runs programs in two or three places, the spreadsheets multiply and never quite match.

**What works instead:** one account with multiple centres, each with its own programs, staff and kiosk, and reports that roll up across all of them.

## The thing you can't compromise on: privacy

Community organizations hold sensitive information about families, children and vulnerable people. Moving off paper should make that data safer, not riskier.

**Look for:**

- role-based access
- PIN-protected sign-in
- encryption in transit and at rest
- clarity on where data is stored and how the software handles Canadian privacy law
